> Replace this quote block with one sentence that captures the core point of the article.

## Why I Wrote This

Write 2 to 4 sentences to explain:

- what this article is about
- why this topic matters
- what kind of reader should keep reading

## Project Background

Use this section to explain the context:

- What was the product or project?
- What problem were you trying to solve?
- What was your role?

Example prompts:

- This project was built for...
- The main problem was...
- I was mainly responsible for...

## What I Built

Break the work into 2 to 4 clear parts.

### 1. Module or Feature One

Describe:

- what you implemented
- why it was needed
- what technologies you used

### 2. Module or Feature Two

Describe:

- how it worked
- any design choices you made
- how it connected to the rest of the system

## Technical Decisions

This is one of the most valuable parts for interviewers.

Write about decisions like:

- why you chose one framework or database
- why you used async processing, queues, caching, SSE, OCR, etc.
- why the final design was better than a simpler alternative

You can use a structure like:

- **Option A:** ...
- **Option B:** ...
- **Final choice:** ...
- **Reason:** ...

## Challenges and Fixes

Write 1 to 3 real problems you ran into.

Examples:

- performance bottlenecks
- asynchronous task flow issues
- data consistency problems
- deployment or SEO issues
- AI output quality or prompt instability

For each one, explain:

1. what happened
2. why it was a problem
3. how you solved it

## What I Learned

Summarize what this project taught you.

Good angles:

- engineering lessons
- product lessons
- collaboration lessons
- what you would do differently next time

## Closing

End with 2 to 3 sentences.

You can write:

- what you want to explore next
- how this project changed your thinking
- how it connects to your future work
